# Ethical boundaries
A score records one encounter: one model, one declared configuration, one sequence of text. It shows how much probability that model assigned to the tokens that appeared. The score belongs to that encounter. It does not belong to the person who wrote the passage.

- Do not treat high surprisal as proof of resistance, embodiment,
  or meaning. It is a probability under one model, and naming it something else
  is an interpretation you are responsible for.
- Do not upload private, confidential, copyrighted, or third-party material
  without permission.

## What is shared here

This repository contains the procedure, its documentation, and a few examples.
My own corpus passages are not included. They remain in the thesis, under the conditions in which they were written.
Everything in examples/ was written only to demonstrate the method.

## Keep the claim close

A score can make a pattern visible: a rhythm, repetition, syntax, segmentation, or moment of model expectation. It cannot tell you what a sentence means or where it came from.
Use it as a reason to read again, not as a conclusion.
